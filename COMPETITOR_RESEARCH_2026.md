# MARROW Psychology — AI / Faceless Competitor Benchmark

**Research date:** 15 November 2026  
**Purpose:** inform the zero-subscription TubeAutoma roadmap. This is a competitive and engineering benchmark, not a promise of views, monetization, or earnings.

## Evidence rules

- A channel having AI-looking visuals does not prove it uses AI narration.
- A creator's case study is self-reported unless independently audited.
- Third-party estimates for monetization and revenue are not YouTube-confirmed.
- Public subscriber and view counts are snapshots and change over time.
- A successful automated workflow is evidence of production capability, not evidence that the channel has product-market fit.

## Relevant examples

### 1. Faceless relationship-psychology channel case study — AI voice confirmed

An operator's public portfolio reports a faceless long-form relationship-psychology channel with:
- A breakout video at 115.1K views and 1.1M impressions at 7.3% CTR.
- Three leading videos contributing 13.7K watch hours and +4.8K subscribers.
- 9–13 minute list-style explainers, AI narration using ElevenLabs, a consistent high-contrast three-line thumbnail format, and weekly review of YouTube Reach data.
- The channel name is withheld, and the metrics are presented by the operator, not independently audited here.

**What we should learn:** AI narration was a production method, not the differentiator. A focused relationship question, clear packaging, a story that sustains 9–13 minutes, and regular performance review were the reported levers. MARROW should borrow the audience-first packaging, not copy exaggerated relationship claims or listicle scripts.

Source: https://www.shahedulhaqueshihab.com/portfolio/relationship-psychology-channel

### 2. Payuu (@ishqbaazzz) — fast-growing relationship-psychology channel; AI imagery classified, voice not verified

FameX's 14 November 2026 snapshot reports:
- 12.1K subscribers, 3.4M lifetime views and 58 videos.
- +1.6K subscribers and approximately 476K views in the preceding seven days.
- Median views of 21.5K across the last 20 uploads and about 2.8 uploads per week.
- Hindi female-psychology and relationship-advice positioning, with recurring attraction/relationship topics and video lengths around 40 minutes.
- FameX classifies the thumbnails as AI-generated imagery. Its report does not verify that the narration is synthetic.

**What we should learn:** a recognisable subject promise and repeatable topic clusters can build audience habit. We should test emotionally recognisable situations, but explain them accurately and without gender stereotypes, manipulation tactics, or claims that a single behaviour proves intent.

Source: https://famexniches.online/c/ishqbaazzz  
**Evidence caveat:** view/subscriber counts are public-data snapshots; AI classification is FameX's analysis; monetization and revenue estimates are not YouTube-confirmed.

### 3. PsycLOGY (@psyclogy) — psychology / relationship advice; AI imagery classified, voice not verified

FameX's 14 November 2026 snapshot reports:
- 35.5K subscribers, 6.1M lifetime views and 95 videos.
- Approximately 189K views and +200 subscribers in the preceding seven days.
- About 0.5 uploads per week, 14-minute median length, and 9K median views over the last 20 uploads.
- A recurring "signs of true love" / psychology and relationship-advice format.
- FameX classifies the imagery as AI-generated; it does not establish AI narration.

**What we should learn:** a coherent catalogue of longer videos can continue to attract viewers without daily uploads. This supports testing one strong long-form anchor each week rather than increasing frequency before quality and audience response justify it.

Source: https://famexniches.online/c/psyclogy  
**Evidence caveat:** all performance figures are third-party snapshots; monetization estimates are not official.

### 4. IndMinds AI (@IndMindsEdu) — disclosed AI documentary pipeline with synthetic voice and human checkpoints

In its own case study, IndMinds reports 122,541 views, 3,128 watch hours and +914 subscribers during 11–17 October 2026. The operator says the first episode of its "Ancient Chronicles" series contributed 57.5% of that week's views. The full-stack write-up describes:
- Local LLM-generated scripts.
- AI-generated scene images animated with pan/zoom.
- An open neural TTS voice.
- Automated assembly and an 11-stage production pipeline.
- Human approval of the script and final upload.
- A local 16 GB NVIDIA GPU as the default free/local setup; cloud alternatives also exist.

The result is the operator's own Studio-based case study, not an independent audit. The hardware requirement also means this is not truly zero-cost for someone without suitable local compute.

**What we should learn:** the series concept and packaging did more work than the mere fact that the workflow was automated. A distinct series, an underserved language/audience gap, appropriate visuals and QA matter. The human approval checkpoints are part of the disclosed operating model.

Sources:
- https://indminds.com/blog/ai-video-channel-1-2-lakh-views-7-days
- https://indminds.com/blog/ai-youtube-channel-1-22-lakh-views-7-days-full-stack

### 5. Spanish faceless psychology marketplace example — AI voice reported, weak repeat conversion

A Fameswap marketplace listing describes a Spanish-language faceless psychology channel with a recurring black-and-white cartoon character and AI voice narration. The listing reports 1,632 subscribers and 359,241 lifetime views, but says one video accounts for 349,205 views. It reports only 2,203 views and +3 subscribers during the preceding 28 days and $7.05 estimated revenue over 12 months.

This is seller-provided marketplace information and not independently verified.

**What we should learn:** one viral video, a large lifetime view total or even a claimed YPP status does not establish a durable audience or meaningful income. Returning viewers and repeatable subscriber conversion matter.

Source: https://app.fameswap.com/listing/427551-youtube-account-for-sale

### 6. Adavia Davis / TubeGen — AI automation at scale, but not a zero-cost or safe template

Fortune's 30 December 2025 profile describes a network of five active faceless channels using AI scripts and visuals, ElevenLabs narration and a proprietary automation pipeline. It reports the operator's claim of roughly $40K–$60K monthly revenue across the network and says Fortune reviewed analytics screenshots and AdSense payout records. The article also describes copycat saturation, high-volume AI output and deceptive watch-time tricks.

**What we should learn:** automation can lower production bottlenecks, but this is a multi-channel, capital-intensive operation with paid tools and oversight costs. Do not copy deceptive retention tricks, copied/recycled material, or a generic content-farm model. Our differentiator must be trustworthy educational value and coherent editorial identity.

Source: https://fortune.com/2025/12/30/ai-slop-faceless-youtube-accounts-adavia-davis-user-generated-content/

### 7. Autonomous AI-agent experiment — automation alone did not convert viewers into subscribers

A creator's February 2026 report says two autonomous agents published 52 videos in six weeks, with 30,170 views and 29 subscribers. The creator still reviewed ideas and video quality and set strategic direction.

**What we should learn:** an agent can research, write, render, upload and analyse. That alone does not produce subscriber growth. The creator's own result is a useful warning against treating upload count or automation autonomy as the business outcome.

Source: https://dev.to/wcamon/i-let-ai-agents-run-my-youtube-channel-for-6-weeks-heres-what-actually-happened-21b1/

## Platform facts that should shape our system

### YouTube's own performance framework

YouTube frames performance in three categories:
1. Appeal — did viewers choose to watch?
2. Engagement — did they stay?
3. Satisfaction — did they enjoy the experience?

YouTube recommends accessible problem-led ideation, compelling and accurate titles/thumbnails, and an opening that immediately delivers the promise. It explicitly says tags are not essential for discovery and are mainly useful for spelling variations.

Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559650

### Monetization risk

YouTube's policy says repetitive or mass-produced "inauthentic" content is ineligible. Repeated templates, interchangeable videos, low-value slideshows and generic AI templates without original insight are examples of risk. Reviewers may consider the channel's main theme, most-viewed and newest videos, watch-time leaders, metadata and About section.

Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392

### Analytics APIs we are not fully using yet

- The current TubeAutoma Analytics Engine collects views, watch minutes, average view duration, average percentage viewed and subscribers gained for top videos.
- The YouTube Analytics API supports audience-retention metrics such as audienceWatchRatio over elapsedVideoTimeRatio. This lets us see the portions of a video where viewers stay or drop off.
- The YouTube Reporting API has a daily Reach Basic report named channel_reach_basic_a1 with video_thumbnail_impressions and video_thumbnail_impressions_ctr. A Reach Combined report adds traffic-source and device dimensions. It is a bulk-report workflow and is distinct from our existing targeted Analytics query.

Official documentation:
- https://developers.google.com/youtube/analytics/metrics
- https://developers.google.com/youtube/reporting/v1/reports/channel_reports
- https://developers.google.com/youtube/reporting/v1/

## Decisions for MARROW Psychology

1. **Keep one channel promise:** evidence-aware explanations of the psychology behind everyday situations. Do not drift into generic dark-psychology myths or unrelated curiosity topics.
2. **Package the problem, explain the concept inside:** use a title such as "Why a delayed reply can feel like rejection" rather than relying only on a textbook term such as "The Spotlight Effect Explained".
3. **Build recurring editorial series:** "Why Your Brain Does That", "The Social Mind", "Memory Is Not a Recording", "Relationship Psychology Without the Myths", and "Habits & Decisions". Each episode must answer a genuinely different question.
4. **Use a weekly long-form anchor and four companion Shorts as a controlled 90-day experiment:** long-form target around 9–13 minutes; Shorts are separate mini-explainers in the same weekly topic cluster, not duplicate cuts.
5. **Upgrade the visuals:** retain local no-cost generation, but mix scene-specific diagrams, small motion graphics, object metaphors and properly licensed free stock. Avoid repeating one static stick figure on every scene. Visuals must directly match the sentence being narrated.
6. **Keep synthetic narration but humanise its performance:** use a quick hook, natural pauses, emphasis on reveals, varied pacing, clean loudness and no robotic monotone. Do not pay for voice APIs.
7. **Build a competitor outlier radar using the official YouTube Data API:** track a small set of relevant search queries and recent public videos; calculate views/day and views relative to the channel's own recent baseline. Use this to identify emerging topic clusters, then produce original angles supported by scholarly sources. Do not scrape or copy scripts, thumbnails or titles.
8. **Add first-party growth analytics:** use the Analytics API for video engagement and retention curves, plus the Reporting API Reach Basic/Combined reports for impressions and CTR. Compare like with like (Shorts versus Shorts, long-form versus long-form, similar video age).
9. **Optimise for subscriber conversion and repeat viewing, not vanity views:** measure subscribers per 1,000 views, returning viewers, watch time per impression where available, and topic-series follow-on performance.
10. **Retain a safety/quality gate:** automated checks should block unsupported claims, repeated scripts, missing narration, mismatched/low-quality visuals, broken audio/video, rights uncertainty and incorrect destination. Public automation must never bypass those checks.

## 30-day experiment after the private pilot is approved

- Week 1: publish one long-form anchor and three or four distinct Shorts in one topic cluster.
- Week 2: make sequels only for angles showing meaningful viewer response; test one different cluster for comparison.
- Week 3: use retention curves to diagnose opening and mid-video drop-offs. Change the writing and visual plan rather than simply swapping tags.
- Week 4: compare views/day, impressions CTR, average view duration/percentage, subscriber gain per 1,000 views and returning-viewer indicators. Keep the series that produces repeat engagement; retire formats that repeatedly underperform.

This is an experiment, not a forecast or monetization guarantee. Free services and quotas can change. The pipeline must stop rather than silently switch to a paid provider.
